Featured Research · Spring 2026

Pricing assets fairly: a research agenda for narrowing the information gap.

EquitAI is an open academic initiative developing machine-learning systems that predict asset values with high accuracy — and explicitly optimize for fairness, so the gains from better forecasting do not accrue only to those who already hold capital.

About the Initiative

An open inquiry into fairer markets.

Established 2024 · Open-source under CC‑BY‑SA

EquitAI was founded on a deceptively simple question: what would financial markets look like if every participant — from a retail investor in rural Kenya to the largest sovereign wealth fund — had access to forecasts of equal quality? The initiative brings together economists, machine-learning researchers, and ethicists to build, test, and openly publish models that are evaluated not only on predictive accuracy, but on whether the gains they produce are distributed equitably across market participants.

Our working hypothesis is that information asymmetry is one of the principal mechanisms by which financial markets concentrate wealth. By releasing model weights, training data, and evaluation code under permissive licences, we aim to make state-of-the-art pricing tools a public good rather than a proprietary advantage.

Four pillars of the research programme.

i.

Multimodal data fusion

Transformer and graph-based architectures trained jointly on price series, earnings transcripts, satellite imagery, and on-chain transactions.

ii.

Equity-first objective

Loss functions that explicitly penalize models whose deployment would increase wealth concentration, drawn from the algorithmic-fairness literature.

iii.

Global coverage

A 15-year dataset spanning 127 countries and 42 asset classes, including emerging-market instruments often omitted from major indices.

iv.

Reproducible & open

All weights, code, and datasets released under CC-BY-SA. Reproducible on consumer-grade GPUs through a distilled student model.

The Problem

Information asymmetry, in numbers.

The institutional pricing apparatus — proprietary data feeds, in-house models, privileged execution venues — produces a measurable accuracy gap between the well-resourced and everyone else. The result is a structural transfer of wealth that operates largely outside public scrutiny.

68%

of global wealth held by the top decile, attributable in significant part to superior market timing and asset selection.

Credit Suisse, Global Wealth Report (2025).

41%

higher mean prediction error among retail traders compared with institutional desks, controlling for asset class and horizon.

EquitAI internal analysis (2025).

3.2×

more likely for low-income households to realize losses during market dislocations than the top quintile.

World Bank Inequality Database (2024).

“The asymmetry is not a feature of capital; it is a feature of access to forecasts. Close that gap and you change the distribution of returns.”

— Working paper, §1.4
Methodology

A model designed to be both accurate and equitable.

We combine a multimodal transformer encoder with a causal graph neural network across 8,472 asset nodes. Critically, training incorporates an adversarial fairness term drawn from Hardt et al. (2016), which limits the model's ability to exploit signals that disproportionately benefit high-net-worth participants.

01

Multimodal data fusion

Prices, earnings transcripts, satellite imagery, social sentiment, weather, and geopolitical events compressed into a single latent representation.

02

Fairness-constrained training

Adversarial fairness terms in the loss limit reliance on signals that systematically favour the already-wealthy.

03

Causal graph neural networks

Markets modelled as evolving graphs, with message-passing learning causal — not merely correlational — relationships between assets.
